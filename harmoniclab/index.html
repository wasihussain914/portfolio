01 · Research

Where hearing meets immunity.

Overview

The HARMONIC Laboratory uses a combination of techniques, including fluorescent imaging, flow cytometry, and deep neural network analysis of facial grimace and movement, to understand how the immune system and neurons of the cochlea interact after exposure to loud noise.

The long-term goal: to develop interventions that prevent or treat noise-induced hearing loss, and associated conditions such as pain hyperacusis.

Active research areas

01 Behavior · Neuro-immunology

A Mouse Model of Pain Hyperacusis

Pain hyperacusis (noxacusis) makes ordinary levels of sound painful. Its mechanism is unknown and there is no current treatment. Because many people can recall a loud noise at the onset of symptoms, we use noise exposure to induce hyperacusis in mice, then quantify cochlear immune cells and type II afferent neuron synapse morphology.

up to50%

of people with pain hyperacusis recall an instance of loud noise associated with the onset of their symptoms.

02 Innate immunity · Repair

The Immune Response to Acoustic Trauma

The macrophage response to acoustic trauma is well documented, but in other systems macrophages are only one part of the immune response to tissue damage. Examining the immune compartment of the inner ear, we found type II innate immune cells to be among the dominant cell types besides macrophages, a pathway that aids wound healing and regeneration but can also drive fibrosis, and one that remains understudied in the ear.

Featured assay · Research area 01

BIOMAP.

A Behavioral Inventory of Mouse Affective Pain

To study sound-evoked pain, you first need a way to show reliably that an animal is in pain in response to sound. BIOMAP uses machine learning to quantify the facial grimace, movement, and complex behaviors of a mouse.

Mice exposed to sound at the human pain level demonstrate changes in behavior equivalent to those at the peak of a migraine.

    A reliable readout of sound-evoked pain, the first step toward an animal model of pain hyperacusis.

Principal Investigator

Dept. of Hearing and Speech Sciences
Vanderbilt University Medical Center

Megan Beers Wood, Ph.D.

Dr. Beers Wood combines her training in immunology with postdoctoral work in auditory neuroscience to study basic mechanisms of hearing. Her interest in research began very early, through her experiences with juvenile rheumatoid arthritis at academic medical centers; in college, her mentor Dr. Kimberly Klonowski helped turn that interest into a career.

Her Ph.D. under Dr. Ifor Williams gave her the tools to study immunology and specialized epithelial cell biology. During her postdoctoral training she found another research interest: the cochlea. Now leading her own lab, she is excited to blend the two, and she is passionate about teaching, through mentoring, the creativity that science requires.
